How Konjure Analytics works

Konjure Analytics builds player projections and grades from opportunity: how much of a team's work a player actually gets. Snaps, routes, targets and carries in football; minutes, usage and shot volume in basketball; plate appearances in baseball. Opportunity is steadier than last week's box score, so it is where the projections start.

The weekly report card

Every quarterback, running back, receiver and tight end with a real role is graded 0-100 on four things: efficiency (EPA per play), production, share of the team's work, and mistakes. Each is compared with every player-week from last season at the same position, so a 90 means better than 90% of them. Grades are final once the week's last game is in, which is why they publish on Tuesday.

The boards, and how they are graded

The NFL board (spread picks, a touchdown scorer board and the parlays) is saved on Friday afternoon, before any game it covers. The NBA board is saved the same way for one night's slate. Nothing is edited afterwards: if a line moves, the published pick stays as published. Each is graded from the official box score and added to the public record.

What we do not claim

  • We do not claim to beat the sportsbooks. Our model's picks have not beaten the closing line, and the record says so.
  • Where the board shows a probability, it is the sportsbook's own price with its margin removed, with our model shown beside it. When the two disagree, the market has usually been right.
  • Parlays are only published when their price clears break-even; the ones that do not are still logged and graded.
  • "Lock", "guaranteed" and similar words are blocked by the publishing system itself.

Live tests

A few betting rules are being tested in the open with fixed rules set in advance, such as taking the under when a player's recent form sits below the line. They are recorded at the odds actually offered and reported with their uncertainty on the record page. A test is not a recommendation.
